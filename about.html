About the Conference

Our Conference on Fair and Explainable AI and Data Science brings together researchers, machine learning engineers, and policy experts to address the dual challenges of algorithmic bias and black-box decision-making in modern AI systems. The conference focuses on technical methodologies, mathematical frameworks, and practical tools designed to ensure automated systems operate transparently, equitably, and reliably across critical domains.

Research Themes

Conference Tracks

Authors may submit to the track that best reflects the primary contribution of their work. Interdisciplinary submissions spanning multiple tracks are welcome.

Track 1

Computational Approaches for Fair, Explainable, and Trustworthy AI

Methods, algorithms, models, and computational frameworks for developing fair, explainable, interpretable, transparent, robust, and trustworthy AI systems, including fairness-aware learning, bias detection and mitigation, interpretability, uncertainty estimation, and trustworthy AI architectures.

Track 2

Fairness and Explainability in Graph AI and Social Networks

Fairness, transparency, and interpretability in social networks, graph-based systems, and networked data, including fairness-aware graph mining, graph neural networks, graph representation learning, graph embeddings, community detection, social network analysis, and responsible learning on interconnected data.

Track 3

Fairness and Explainability in Vision AI and Image Processing

Fairness, transparency, interpretability, and robustness in image processing and computer vision, including explainable vision models, fairness-aware visual learning, interpretable deep vision, bias detection in image datasets, visual reasoning, image classification, object recognition, segmentation, and trustworthy vision systems.

Track 4

Fairness and Explainability in Language AI and NLP

Fairness, transparency, interpretability, and trustworthiness in natural language processing and language models, including fairness-aware NLP, explainable language models, large language models, bias detection and mitigation, responsible language generation, multilingual fairness, hallucination, and trustworthy generative AI.

Track 5

Human-Centered Fair and Explainable AI

Human interaction, user-centered evaluation, and cognitive aspects of fair and explainable AI, including human–AI interaction, explanation quality, user trust, behavioral evaluation, cognitive perspectives, human feedback, AI-assisted decision-making, and responsible human–AI collaboration.

Track 6

Applications of Fair and Explainable AI

Applications of fair, explainable, and trustworthy AI across real-world domains, including healthcare, finance, education, public services, science, engineering, cybersecurity, business, industry, and other high-impact applications, with emphasis on responsible deployment and real-world impact.

Paper Categories

Accepted submission types

  • Full Paper: 12–15 pages (or more)
  • Short Paper: 6–11 pages

Formatting & Page Limits

Springer Proceedings Format

  • Use the official Springer proceedings LaTeX or Microsoft Word template.
  • Full Papers: 12–15 pages (or more).
  • Short Papers: 6–11 pages.
  • Figures, tables, references, and headings should follow the Springer template.
  • Papers should contain significant scientific content.
  • Final accepted papers may require editable source files and a signed License-to-Publish form.

Review Process

Single-blind peer review

Submissions will be reviewed based on originality, technical quality, relevance to fair and explainable AI and data science, methodological rigor, clarity of presentation, and significance of the contributions. Reviewers will also assess the soundness of the evaluation, reproducibility, and potential scientific or societal impact.

Originality & Plagiarism Policy

Original and unpublished work

Submissions must represent original work that has not been published and is not simultaneously submitted elsewhere. Manuscripts may be screened for textual overlap and plagiarism. Ethical or publication-policy violations may result in rejection or withdrawal from the proceedings.
